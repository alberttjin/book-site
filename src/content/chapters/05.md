As they approached the shore, Jin heard a noise he hadn’t heard in a while.


Chatter. 


Thousands of men had gathered on the shore. Their varied stature, hair color, and muscularity provided no sense of unity and yet, Jin knew this was the full might of this island’s northern defense force. 


The moment the crowd turned and saw Yuna, they fell silent. 


Hundreds of heads bowed as one.


Yuna’s deep, belting voice broke the silence.


“The approaching island is W. 


A murmur passed through the ranks. 


“This will not be a pleasant encounter. Worst case, prepare for war. Collisions have been lasting longer. Conserve your energy. Do not engage in wasteful heroics, prioritize your life. Follow orders. I will take care of the rest.”


The men erupted in cheers.


However, as the island loomed closer, the cheers and chatter slowly died into a dreary silence. The high mountain cliffs on each side cast a long shadow into the shores of B island. Meanwhile, the enemy island stretched miles past the east and west limits of their own. But most importantly, on the opposite shore, thousands upon thousands of soldiers dotted the beach a uniformly black color. Drowning out even the nervous chirps of nearby birds, their screams instilled a heavy fear in Yuna’s army. 


As the excitement slowly became corrupted, a cloud of negativity started to spread through pockets of the army.


“Can we win this?”


“She only became the leader recently…”


“This is her first big war, isn’t it?”


Noticing the restlessness of her army, Finn turned to Yuna with a concerned look.


“Ah-”


“It’s fine.” Yuna interrupted. “No need to rile them up yet. War has yet to start”. Yuna stared off into the distance, narrowing her eyes, as if zooming in on a single point on the enemy island. 


A group of older men separated themselves from the army as they approached. Donning similar blue uniforms with small golden medals pinned to their left breast pocket, Jin could tell they were of a higher rank than the rest of the army. Leading the pack was a particularly seasoned looking veteran. His bushy white beard and suntanned wrinkles suggested an age that did not befit his full muscles bulging out of his suit. His large scars seemingly pulsated with memories of battles past. 


“Greetings, Commander. My name is–”
“Brigadier General Alden. What’s up?” 


Alden blinked, pausing for a second.


“I-I just wanted to know. Are we striking a deal?”


Yuna stared into his eyes intensely.


“Depends.” 


Alden paused, waiting for more details that never came.


“I might be out of line, but we have to strike a deal. This is not a war we can win. It’s better to lose civilians to starvation than capable soldiers to war. We must conserve our power.”


Yuna smiled. The men emanated distrust while maintaining a phony air of respect. Yuna knew the age and experience gap was not something she could overcome with words alone.


“I’ll see what I can do.”


Unsatisfied, but knowing there was not much more they could get out of her, the men returned to the army. 


After another half hour of nervous preparation, the hour was finally upon them. 


“PREPARE FOR IMPACT. GET DOWN.” Yuna belted.


LIke the world’s largest game of dominoes, soldiers on both islands alike lay flat on the ground, putting out all campfires and securing sharp objects and weapons. Looking up, they all collectively stared at the same thing.


The collision point. 


This was where the islands would connect, and stay connected for the next day. Often, this strongly influenced battle strategies and in turn, whether they lived or died. For the clearly disadvantaged island B, they would be playing defense and they wanted one thing, and one thing only–a singular narrow bridge. Lacking the resources and technology to make large fleets of boats, armies often invaded by land only, and a narrow bridge would help offset their smaller numbers.


BOOM.


The islands made contact. The ground shook as birds screeched, taking flight into the refuge of the sky. The barren trees lost what few leaves they had managed to grow as the deep reverberation spread through the island, shaking even the villages located close to the Southside of the island. 


As the islands settled, a silence befell as all soldiers looked up with bated breath. 


Shit.


A bridge had formed. But it was not narrow. It was wide enough for over a thousand soldiers to come pouring in at the same time. And even worse–there were two. The island had made contact in two points. And they were over half a km apart. 


Defending two large chokeholds with half the army size. An impossible task. If they split their army up, they would be overrun by the side the enemy chose to focus. If they chose one and picked wrong, the enemy soldiers would pour into their island, ransacking their grain storehouses and stealing the resources they had undoubtedly came for. Even worse, if the enemy were to split their army, they could fight a stalemate at one chokehold while invading the other, flanking, and destroying Island B’s army from behind. 


Soldiers dropped their weapons, mouths agape. They clutched their stomachs as they imagined spears, swords and other weaponry relentlessly cutting down their comrades to the left and right. In their minds, they had already lost. They hoped, with all their heart, the enemy would be merciful, striking a deal that would spare their lives. 


Some more experienced soldiers, however, sharpened their blades and their minds, preparing for the fight they knew was coming.


Jin glanced at Yuna’s face. She had not moved an inch. Not during the collision. And not now. She made no attempt to quell the army’s rapidly declining morale. Her face remained stoic, and her earlier playful, sadistic nature was nowhere to be found, instead replaced with a quiet, eerie confidence. Her stare remained fixated off into the distance.


Following her eyeline, Jin landed upon three men in the distance, who had set foot onto the left bridge, closest to where they were located. 


In the middle, was a man of incredible stature. Hard pressed to even call him the same species, Jin had never seen a man that large. His minimal clothing set made no effort to hide columns of solid muscle and his long unruly hair was knotted with blood and dirt. 


To his right, a stark contrast of an old man hobbled along, barely keeping pace with the long strides of the hulking giant. Holding a flask in his left hand, it was easy to understand where the large grin on his face was coming from. 


Lastly, on the left side, a young man wore a dark assassin’s robe and hood. 


As the men approached, Yuna finally moved to greet them. The army stood frozen as they knew it was customary for commanders to meet, attempting a trade deal before engaging in violent blows. However, this custom was not all that kept them to the ground. 


The man’s sheer confidence. Completely relaxed, not a tense muscle in his body. Head held high, eyes facing forward, without even a glance at the enemies surrounding him. As if they were nothing. This army of several thousand–nothing. 


“Yuna.” 


Jin searched her greeting for the slightest flicker of hesitation. A faint tremor passed through her left hand, so brief he almost wondered if he’d imagined it. Not bad.  


“Zaheen.”


Their intros were short, but that was all that was needed. The rest would be said in private.


Yuna had brought them to a smaller hut, a few steps into the forest. There sat a rather large rectangular table and on one side sat Yuna, Lilith and Finn while Zaheen and his two lieutenants sat opposed.


Arms crossed, Zaheen looked even more intimidating in person.


“What happened to the other man?” Zaheen melted the tense frigid air with his fiery voice.


“He died. I took over.” Yuna responded matter of factly. 


“Hmm.” Zaheen looked Yuna up and down, before clicking his tongue dismissively.


“30%. Give us 30% of your grain storage for this winter. That’s it. No other resources. And we’ll let this collision pass without incident.” The man held up three of his large sausage fingers.


“You act like you’re doing us a favor.” 


Zaheen looked at her in disbelief.


“Have you seen our army? Have you seen the collision point? Is this your first day on the job?”


Zaheen sat back in his chair before letting a small smirk take over his face. He slowly and dramatically lifted one more finger.


“40%. Purely because that was disrespectful”


Yuna smiled.


“You can play with numbers all you’d like. You have no intention of making a deal. We all know it.”


Meanwhile, Jin had perched himself on the bare branches of a rather tall tree. He closed his eyes, leaned against the trunk and attempted to relax. The salty tension in the air. The sounds of swords being sharpened. The sweaty smell of thousands of men. None of it bothered him. This was not his island. This was his vacation. 


But he couldn’t sleep.


Opening his eyes, he heard an odd rustling below him. Barely camouflaged among the sparse brushes was a small boy, no older than 10 or 11. He sat crouched, gripping tightly a set of daggers reminiscent of Yuna’s twin swords. His eyes were laser focused on the negotiation headquarters, waiting for movement of any kind. 


Jin looked a bit closer.


An insignia. A feather insignia. On the boy’s dagger. He too? He was an artifact holder? A kid this young? What was with this island?


At that moment, the door to the negotiation HQ swung open. Zaheen emerged, ducking under the frame of the door. His two lieutenants followed. 


The boy’s muscles tensed. 


Zaheen walked toward the shore. Thundering one step and a time until he was directly parallel from the boy.


The boy lept. His small legs whipped back and forth as he raised his speed. Reaching forward, he thrust his dagger, aiming for Zaheen’s kneecap. 


But his speed, his power, nothing was close to enough. Calmly, Zaheen noticed the boy’s approach and thrust out his shield. 


The boy’s dagger glowed. With a big swing directly at the shield, he cut through the shield like butter. Zaheen’s eyes widened, noticing the insignia on the boy’s weapon. Swiftly shifting his body to the right, he attempted to dodge. However, the boy’s dagger caught the edge of Zaheen’s leg, ripping through a couple inches of flesh, leaving a gashing wound, but nothing deep enough to cause a real injury. 


Landing after his strike, the boy turned around for one more.


“ZEB. PUT DOWN YOUR WEAPON”
Yuna stared daggers at the young boy, steam practically rising from the heat that had filled her face. 


“But… we can end this here, Commander! We can save so many lives!” Zeb retorted rather meekly.


Yuna reinforced her steely stare.


Zeb dropped his daggers and lowered his head.


Turning to Zaheen. Yuna drew her sword. 


Without a word, she slowly brought it down toward her own leg, mirroring the exact position of Zaheen’s injury. 


Blood surged down Yuna’s leg. 


Zaheen snorted with a hint of admiration. 


“See you on the battlefield.” Zaheen waved as the three of them made their way back to their island.
