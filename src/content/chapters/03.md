Yuna led Jin through a small forest, marching ahead while Jin trudged at a pace designed to irritate her.


Her earlier decree that he was not to leave her sight had done little to improve his mood. Every step rattled his wounds and every impatient glance she threw over her shoulder only tempted him to slow down further.


What rotten luck. 


As they strolled in silence, Jin looked around and took notice of something odd. The forest, the ground, even the air that hung above the island seemed … off. The dirt was so dry it might as well have been sand. The trees stood stripped of leaves in the middle of the summer and seemed to have stopped growing prematurely. The air itself felt stale.


Finally, the view gave way to a clearing hosting a small village. 


Weathered cottages sat clustered around narrow dirt roads, their roofs and paneling patched with blocks of wood ranging in shades of brown. Similar to the Defense Force headquarters, small vegetable gardens bordered each home, but large patches of empty earth between the small crops spoke of hard times ahead.


The villagers tended to their garden with the quiet rhythm of people long accustomed to hardship. Their faces held little emotion beyond the weariness carved into them by years of scraping by. 


Breaking the silence, a small boy ran about humming a familiar tune while occasionally squatting next to various adults, pestering them with questions in an attempt to keep himself entertained.


As Yuna descended the hill with her bucket of fish, the villagers began to crowd her with a cheerful murmur. The boy wiggled through the pack in an attempt to peer into Yuna’s pail. 


“How many fish today?” the boy asked with a glimmer in his big round eyes. His tousled hair blew in the wind as he waddled forward, paying no attention to the surrounding adults and teenagers who had taken notice of Jin. Although they stared with intrigue for it was not every day they encountered an outsider, they oddly did not bother to ask Yuna who the man was.


“Enough for you to take one home, Asta.” Yuna gave the young boy a soft pat on the head while Jin watched her smile warmly, contrasting the devilish grin he had gotten used to. She continued to pass out fish to the villagers, attempting to distribute them evenly as one per household. The elderly thanked her with a respectful bow before briskly walking back to their hut, rolling up their sleeves and getting to work on breakfast. 


Eventually, one fish remained and the crowd had cleared. Yuna stretched and let out an audible contented hum before letting her face drop back into the loose dissatisfaction Jin was used to facing. 


“Leader, wait!” A rather high pitched voice rang through the air as Jin attempted to make out the silhouette of a man in the distance. As he approached, Jin noted his peculiar appearance. A full head of grey hair, eyebags, and broken bifocals directly contradicted his youthful step and overall limber way of moving. He wore a plastered smile, one that never quite reached his tired eyes. 


“Do you have one more to spare? I know I’m late, but Rebecca’s been feeling quite ill all morning.”


Jin made note of Yuna’s cheek twitching in hesitation as she looked down at the lone fish left in the pail and up at the man’s pleading face. 


“I only have a single small one left, Frank. You know I can’t.” Yuna failed to meet the man’s eye line, turning slightly to the side, staring at the ground.


The man’s face hardened as the upturned corners of his mouth made their way south. 


“Seriously? You know that’s not gonna help. Just tell your mother the truth. Please, I need it.” Yuna shuffled backwards half a step, kicking up a small cloud of dirt as she avoided eye contact even further. This sight caught Jin off guard. Where was the devilish woman that he had met on the beach? There was no trace of her wild behavior in her slouched, apologetic posture. 


Reading her body language, Frank knew what the answer was. His face became flush as his nose twitched in anger. 


“Fine. Waste it on him. A disgrace, your whole family. Like father, like daughter,” Frank grumbled as he trudged away. 


Jin glanced at Yuna, curious to get a glimpse of her expression as the man walked away. But Yuna, ignoring him, had already set off down the dirt path toward a small hut on the edge of town. Jin followed as he attempted to make out her expression from the back of her head. Was she sad? Defeated? Or simply dealing with business as usual? He had no idea. 


Reaching the house on the corner, Yuna stopped for a brief moment, taking two shallow hiccuped breaths before putting her hand on the door knob and lightly creaking it open. The dim light above revealed a young man in his teenage years sitting at a long wooden table, staring blankly at a small space between the slats. A much older woman was sitting next to him, examining his face, almost as if she was looking for signs of life. In her lap, she simultaneously wove a needle through a set of frayed, dark brown thread–graduallly forming what seemed to be a men’s shirt. 


“I’m home.” Yuna said softly. 


No answer. 


Just like the villagers, the two didn’t acknowledge Jin, but unlike the villagers, it seemed they held no curiosity whatsoever. 


But Yuna did not seem surprised. 
Instead, she walked over to the pantry and grabbed a large bag of rice. Lifting it with ease, she threw it at Jin. Barely catching it, he hobbled back a few steps. 


“Make some porridge,” she demanded. 


Jin’s left eye twitched in irritation. But before he could object, she had already moved on to the kitchen. 


Grabbing a hair tie nearby, she put her silky hair up in a ponytail before donning a stiff, wrinkled apron. Using a small knife, she adeptly dissected the last remaining small fish, cutting two beautiful filets without wasting an ounce of meat. She continued to scrape meat into a dish until all that was left was a spine and a head. Tossing the spine away, she gently lowered the head and meat into a boiling pot of water. Seasoning the water with a seemingly random assortment of leaves and spices, she closed the lid and flipped a rusted hourglass sitting on the kitchen counter. 


Looking over at Jin, she saw that he had barely begun on the porridge. Her eyes narrowed and lips pursed as she used her head to signal toward the pot. 


“What’s wrong? You forgot how to put rice in water?”


Jin’s cheeks swelled with heat. What was wrong with this woman? Who was she to make demands of him? The leader of this dinky island?


Taking a deep breath, Jin calmed himself. Clearly, he had caught her suspicion. He needed to stay rational. But something about this woman bothered him, even more so than his desire to complete the mission that was assigned to him.


Completing the meal, the two set the table. The spread actually looked quite delicious, Yuna had prepared a side of pan fried spinach and garlic to go with the fragrant hot and sour fish soup and porridge. Sitting down, Yuna placed a bowl in front of her mother and her brother. Immediately, her mother began to serve her brother, scooping the entire fish head into his bowl. Using a small spoon, she dug out the brains of the fish before feeding it to her brother. 


Spoonful by spoonful, she fed the young man morsels of meat. 


“Thanks for the food,” her mother said without bothering to make eye contact with Yuna, continuing to feed her son. 


“Of course.” Yuna poured herself a bowl of porridge and began eating.


Jin sat there, awkwardly. Was he to feed himself? He was starving, this was no time for manners.


Grabbing a separate bowl, Jin reached to spoon himself a nice helping of fish soup before Yuna slapped his hand. Giving him a stern look, she shook her finger. 


What? He couldn’t even have a few pieces of fish? After he helped make the food? 


He again attempted to help himself to a few morsels of scrumptious looking fish.


He felt a familiar slap on his hand. Jin’s face flushed with irritation as he brought his stare to meet hers.


“Porridge.”


Jin frowned. “What?”


She nudged the pot of porridge toward him.


“Guests eat porridge.”


“... And the fish?”


“For my brother”.


Jin glanced around the table. Her brother’s bowl brimmed with fish. Her mother’s held a few carefully rationed pieces, spooned for her by Yuna earlier. Yuna’s contained nothing but porridge and some vegetables. 


With a quiet sigh, Jin lowered himself back onto his stool. How was he to complete his mission without protein? He tenderly touched his finger to his right cheek. More importantly, would his skin lose elasticity without the proper nutrients? Jin watched as Yuna wolfed down her porridge.
